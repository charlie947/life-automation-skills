# Document types: what to include, what to leave blank

One entry per document type. Each lists the sections to always include, the facts to leave as `[NEEDS: …]` when the brief is silent, and whether the legal-review line is required.

## Rules that apply to every type

- **Never invent a fact.** Parties, names, figures, fees, dates, durations, jurisdictions, and governing law are supplied by the user or left as `[NEEDS: …]`. Never guess one to make the draft look finished.
- **The review line, verbatim, at the top of every contract or legal output:**
  **"This is a draft, not legal advice. Review it with a qualified professional before you send or sign."**
- **Flag, do not bury.** If you include a standard clause the brief did not mention (liability cap, indemnity, auto-renewal, exclusivity, non-compete, penalty), call it out so a human decides.
- **Label everything a draft.** Even a proposal or internal doc, which does not need the legal-advice line, ships headed DRAFT.

## NDA (non-disclosure / confidentiality agreement)

Legal-review line: **required.**

Always include:
- Parties (disclosing and receiving), or mark it mutual if both sides disclose.
- Definition of "Confidential Information", and what is excluded (already public, independently developed, lawfully received).
- Purpose the information may be used for.
- Obligations of the receiving party.
- Term of the agreement, and how long confidentiality survives after it ends.
- Return or destruction of materials on request.
- Governing law and jurisdiction.
- Signature block.

Leave as `[NEEDS: …]`:
- Party legal names and addresses.
- One-way or mutual.
- Confidentiality period (e.g. 2, 3, 5 years).
- Governing jurisdiction.

## SOW (statement of work)

Legal-review line: **required** (it is usually a binding schedule to a contract).

Always include:
- Parties, and the master agreement it sits under, if any.
- Background / objective.
- Scope of work and specific deliverables.
- Explicit out-of-scope list (what is not included).
- Timeline, milestones, and dependencies.
- Fees, payment schedule, and expenses.
- Acceptance criteria (how a deliverable is signed off).
- Change-control process.
- Term and termination.
- Signature block.

Leave as `[NEEDS: …]`:
- Deliverable specifics and dates.
- Fee amounts and payment triggers.
- Named milestones.
- Acceptance criteria if the brief is vague.

---

## Proposal

Legal-review line: **not required** (it is a pitch, not a binding contract). Still ship it headed DRAFT.

Always include:
- Client and sender.
- Problem / objective in the client's words.
- Proposed approach and scope.
- Deliverables and timeline.
- Pricing and options (tiers, if relevant).
- Assumptions and what is excluded.
- Next steps.
- Validity period of the quote.

Leave as `[NEEDS: …]`:
- Pricing, if the brief did not set it.
- Client-specific objectives you were not told.
- Named dates and durations.

Note: if the proposal is meant to become a signable contract, flag that the pricing and scope will need contract terms and a legal review before signature.

## MSA (master services agreement)

Legal-review line: **required.**

Always include:
- Parties.
- Structure: the MSA sets the terms, individual SOWs define the work.
- Services (framed at the master level).
- Fees and payment terms (general), with specifics deferred to each SOW.
- Intellectual property ownership.
- Confidentiality (or a reference to a separate NDA).
- Warranties and disclaimers.
- Limitation of liability. **Flag this clause explicitly.**
- Indemnification. **Flag this clause explicitly.**
- Insurance, if relevant.
- Term, renewal, and termination.
- Governing law and dispute resolution.
- Signature block.

Leave as `[NEEDS: …]`:
- Party details.
- Liability cap figure or basis.
- Indemnity scope.
- Governing jurisdiction and dispute-resolution venue.
- Renewal terms (or none).

This is the highest-risk type. Every material clause a human has not specified stays a `[NEEDS: …]` or a flagged default. Do not set a liability cap, indemnity, or auto-renewal on your own.

## Service agreement / engagement letter

Legal-review line: **required.**

Always include:
- Parties.
- Services provided.
- Term and termination.
- Fees, payment terms, and expenses.
- Responsibilities of each party.
- Confidentiality.
- Intellectual property / ownership of work.
- Liability and indemnity (flag both).
- Governing law.
- Signature block.

Leave as `[NEEDS: …]`:
- Scope specifics.
- Fees and payment schedule.
- Term length and notice period.
- Jurisdiction.

An engagement letter (professional services) is a lighter, letter-form version of the same content. Keep the substance, drop the heavy contract framing, still carry the review line.

---

## Offer letter (employment / contractor)

Legal-review line: **required** (employment terms are jurisdiction-specific and carry legal risk).

Always include:
- Employer and candidate.
- Role title and reporting line.
- Start date.
- Compensation (salary or rate) and pay frequency.
- Benefits summary, if any.
- Employment type (permanent, fixed-term, contractor) and hours.
- Probation period, if any.
- Confidentiality and IP assignment, if relevant.
- Conditions of the offer (references, right to work, background check).
- Signature / acceptance block.

Leave as `[NEEDS: …]`:
- Candidate name and role details.
- Salary or rate.
- Start date.
- Benefits specifics.
- Notice and probation terms.

Employment law varies sharply by country and region. Flag clearly that a qualified professional must review this before it is sent, and never assert what the law requires in a given place.

---

## A type not listed here

Use the closest entry as a base. Keep the universal rules: include the sections a reader of that document would expect, leave every unsupplied fact as `[NEEDS: …]`, carry the review line on anything legal or financial, and flag any material clause you added by default.
