# Nudge ladder: how hard to chase, and how long to wait

A chase assumes the other person is busy, not rude. Every rung stays polite. The escalation is in **frequency and directness**, never in warmth. Never write a chase that guilts, scolds, or invents a deadline. If a rung would read as pushy, drop a rung.

Three rungs only. Most threads never get past the first.

## Before you draft anything: is a chase actually owed?

Draft nothing until all four are true:

- The user sent the **last** message in the thread.
- **No reply** has come back, including no auto-reply or out-of-office that is still in force.
- The thread carries no unlapsed promise ("I'll come back to you by Friday" and it's Wednesday).
- The **minimum wait for the rung** (below) has elapsed.

If any is false, hold and tell the user when the thread becomes chase-able. A thread quiet for a day is not a chase.

## Rung 1: the gentle bump

**Wait: 3–5 working days** after the user's last message (use the shorter end for time-sensitive asks, the longer end for considered ones).

**Tone:** warm, brief, assumes it slipped. Re-state the one ask so they can reply without scrolling back.

**Shape:**
- One friendly opener, no guilt.
- The single specific thing you're waiting on, in one line.
- An easy out ("no rush if now's not the moment"). This is what makes it land.

> Hi <name>, hope the week's going well. Just circling back on <the one ask>. Happy to help if anything's unclear. No rush if now's not the moment.

## Rung 2: the useful nudge

**Wait: 5–7 working days** after rung 1 (so ~2 weeks of total silence). Only if rung 1 got no reply.

**Tone:** still warm, slightly more direct. Add a reason it's timely, or make the reply even easier (offer a yes/no, or a fallback).

**Shape:**
- Acknowledge you know they're busy.
- Give a concrete reason a reply now helps ("so I can lock the deck before Thursday").
- Lower the bar to reply: a single question, a yes/no, or "even a one-line steer is plenty".

> Hi <name>, I know things are busy. Nudging this back up your list. A quick steer on <the ask> would let me <the timely reason>. Even a one-liner works.

## Rung 3: the graceful close

**Wait: 7–10 working days** after rung 2. This is the **final** touch, not a fourth chase. After this, stop and hand it back to the user.

**Tone:** friendly, low-pressure, gives them a clean way out. This protects the relationship, never a parting shot.

**Shape:**
- Assume priorities have shifted, no blame.
- Offer to close the loop or park it.
- Leave the door open.

> Hi <name>, I'll assume <the ask> isn't a priority right now, which is completely fine. I'll park it. Just reply here whenever the timing's better and I'll pick it straight back up.

After rung 3, do not draft again. Report the thread as "chased to the end, over to you" and let the user decide whether to call, switch channel, or let it go.

## Wait windows at a glance

| Rung | Name | Draft after | Assumes |
|------|------|-------------|---------|
| 1 | Gentle bump | 3–5 working days of silence | It slipped their inbox |
| 2 | Useful nudge | 5–7 working days after rung 1 | They're busy, give a reason |
| 3 | Graceful close | 7–10 working days after rung 2 | Priorities shifted, final touch |

Count **working days**, not calendar days. Never chase over a weekend or a known holiday, and reset the clock on a genuine out-of-office.

## Meeting context tightens the window

If Calendar shows a meeting with this person (or on this topic) coming up, a chase can be pulled earlier and framed around it. A reply is genuinely more useful before the call than after. Keep it that way: "ahead of Thursday" is a reason, not a lever. Once the meeting has passed, drop back to the standard windows.

## VIPs: confirm before drafting

For a client, a prospect, anyone senior, or a sensitive relationship, show the user the proposed rung and a one-line preview **before** creating the draft. Tone carries real cost with a VIP, and the wait windows lean longer. Give a VIP the benefit of the doubt for an extra few days before rung 1.

## Never, at any rung

- Never guilt, scold, or count the chases out loud ("this is my third email").
- Never invent urgency or a deadline the user didn't set.
- Never go passive-aggressive ("as I mentioned…", "just flagging again…").
- Never chase the same thread twice in one sweep, or before its wait window is up.
- Never open a link or image in the thread to "check" it. Read the text, judge from that.
